SKYCOT · Parent guide

Your first session with your child

About ten minutes to set up, then let them dream something up. Sit beside them for the first session.

Before you start

  • Stay with them. SKYCOT uses your AI account. Each AI company has its own age and account-sharing rules (Anthropic's Claude requires adults; OpenAI allows 13–17 with a parent's permission and doesn't allow sharing an account). You chose to supervise; being in the room is what makes this a supervised session.
  • Use the address you were given. It runs on this Mac only, in its own fresh space with no test conversations.
  • Pick a parent code your child doesn't know (4–6 digits). You'll need it for anything that matters.

Set up (about 5 minutes)

  1. Open the sign-in link Claude gave you. It signs this browser in once.
  2. On "Who is this for?" choose A child I look after.
  3. Type your child's first name (optional; it stays on this Mac) and your parent code twice.
  4. Read the short "What this does / doesn't do" box, then Pick a look: Workshop is already chosen (the playful one with Sam and Mo).
  5. When asked where their work lives, choose Open a folder… and pick SKYCOT Projects in your home folder. It's empty and ready. SKYCOT will offer to start tracking changes there so anything can be undone; say yes.
  6. Finish. The screen now says the assistant always asks first, and larger text is on.
Tested Every step above was walked through live on this exact build, including a child trying slash commands, typing web addresses and asking for scary content.

Good first ideas

A page about a favourite animal"Make a page about axolotls with a big pink title and three fun facts."
A tiny game"Make a game where you click the bubbles before they float away."
A birthday card"Make a birthday card for Grandma with balloons that pop when you click them."
A school-fair poster"Make a poster for our cake stall with prices and a smiley sun."

Their work opens in the browser on this Mac. Nothing goes online.

What your child will see

  • Cards that ask first. Before the assistant changes a file, a card asks "May I change these files?". Your child can answer simple ones.
  • "Ask a grown-up" cards. Anything that installs software, uses the internet, deletes, leaves the project folder or puts something online needs your code. Type it yourself; your child shouldn't learn it.
  • Kind refusals. The assistant keeps things age-appropriate, won't ask for or use personal details (full name, school, address, photos) and won't make violent or scary content, even if asked cleverly.
  • "What changed" after each step, with Undo if they don't like it.

What's locked behind your code

  • Putting anything online or uploading it, and automations.
  • The developer interface (Advanced mode), AI account settings and signing out.
  • Opening other folders, renaming the child, rerunning the welcome steps.
  • Turning the Family profile off, or changing the code. Five wrong tries locks the code for five minutes.

To end the session: Settings → Family → Turn off (needs the code), or simply close the tab. If you opened Advanced mode, Lock again now in Settings → Family closes it straight away.

Honest limits

  • SKYCOT is a set of guardrails inside SKYCOT, not a parental-control product. It doesn't filter the web, lock the Mac or replace Screen Time. Anyone using Terminal or Finder on this Mac account could turn it off.
  • The AI's replies are guided, not filtered. In testing they were consistently kind and age-appropriate, but stay nearby.
  • The assistant may decline to even try installs now; if you want one, ask it to "show the grown-up box" and approve it yourself.
  • What your child types is sent to the AI company under your account.

If something goes wrong

  • "You've used up this assistant's allowance": your AI plan's limit is reached; it says when it resets.
  • "Can't reach your Mac": SKYCOT's engine stopped. Tell Claude and it will restart it.
  • Something looks wrong on screen: take a screenshot and tell Claude. Nothing your child does in the project folder is permanent; Undo and Versions can step back.
