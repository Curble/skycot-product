8:30 pm8:30 pm·Questions

Questions before bed.

Is it safe for kids?

It’s designed as a safe first workshop for a child and a grown-up together, the way you’d take a child swimming: you don’t hand them the pool, and you don’t ban water either. You get in with them. SKYCOT is for a child and an adult together; a child must not use it on their own, and the adult stays present and supervising for the whole session.

With the Family profile on, the assistant shows a plan and asks before every change, the riskier modes and developer tools are hidden, Undo is always there, and nothing goes online without the parent code. These are guardrails inside the app, not a security product, and the AI service’s own safety settings and age rules still apply. Look at what was made together before it’s shared.

Does my child need an AI account?

No. SKYCOT runs on a parent’s own Mac and the parent’s own AI account, and the parent sets it up and supervises. Your child doesn’t sign up for anything, with us or with an AI service, and SKYCOT has no account of its own.

What your child types goes to the AI service you chose, under your account and that company’s terms, so check its rules on age and on who may use your account.

What can the parent code lock?

Putting work online (both “send for review” and “put it live”), creating an automation, opening the developer tools (Advanced mode), opening another folder, changing which AI the children use, adding or editing a child, giving more time after bedtime, restarting to update, running the welcome steps again, changing the code, and turning the Family profile off. Five wrong tries locks every code prompt for five minutes.

Everything else works as normal: building, drafts and Undo.

What does SKYCOT not do?

It isn’t a parental-control or security product. It doesn’t filter the web, lock the Mac or replace Screen Time. Its bedtime and daily limits apply only inside SKYCOT. It asks the AI to keep things child-friendly and spots personal details before a message goes, but it can’t guarantee what an AI says. The parent code is kept on your Mac, and a determined teenager could get round it.

SKYCOT also doesn’t create AI accounts for anyone, sell AI time, or send us your work: we count update checks and optional relay connections as anonymous daily totals that reset each UTC day and cannot be linked between days.

Which AI does it use?

The one you already pay for. A grown-up signs in to at least one: ChatGPT (which runs Codex, from OpenAI), Google (Antigravity) or Claude (Claude Code, from Anthropic), with Cursor, Grok and others under “More”. ChatGPT and Google sign in inside SKYCOT; Claude takes two short Terminal commands, once.

Each service sets its own age rules. As of October 2026, Anthropic’s Claude and Cursor require every user to be 18 or over, and OpenAI’s terms allow 13 to 17 year olds with a parent’s permission; Google sets its own rules for Gemini and Antigravity. Check your service’s current terms before your child uses SKYCOT with it.

What does SKYCOT cost?

Free during the beta. You pay your AI provider as you do today. Pricing for later will be announced before it applies to anyone.

Does it work on Windows or iPhone?

Mac first. The beta is for Apple silicon Macs only. The iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch app is being tested with a small group through TestFlight: a parent can approve a request and see the day from their phone, and it needs the family Mac running SKYCOT. It isn’t on the App Store yet. Windows is on the list, and the dates will be honest ones.

Does voice typing send my child's voice anywhere?

Voice typing starts off for every child, and a grown-up turns it on with the parent code. Today it works in the phone app, on the device, and when SKYCOT is opened in Safari on the Mac; it isn’t in the Mac app’s own window yet. SKYCOT never records or keeps audio: the device’s own speech service turns speech into text, and SKYCOT says which one applies before you switch it on. Reading replies aloud uses the Mac’s own voices, so nothing leaves the Mac.
